Takeaway

What the record shows

What three years of saved footage from Gaza adds up to: the story it tells, what keeping it changes, and who can put it to use.

Figures from the 2 November 2026 snapshot of the Archive Genocide records API. The linked pages update with each refresh.

Summary
  • Scale: 186,628 videos and photographs from 31 collections, October 2023 to October 2026. 33,993 carry a map location.
  • The story: the footage moved with the people filming it: north first, then Rafah, then the central camps, then back north.
  • Attention follows events: hospital mentions peak in December 2024, during the siege of Kamal Adwan; "aid" and "famine" peak in August 2025, the month famine was confirmed in Gaza Governorate.[1]
  • Constant subject: "children" is the most frequent caption word, in 20,957 records.
  • Fragile: only 3 of 31 collections are still growing; three collections hold 45% of all records.
  • Use: a dated, searchable, partly mapped index of original posts. A starting point for checking, not a verdict.

1.The narrative

Read in order, the archive is a record of where people could still film and post, month by month. Its shape follows displacement, sieges and hunger.

  1. Oct–Dec 2023. North Gaza and Gaza City hold 47% of mapped records. The Indonesian Hospital's busiest month is November 2023.
  2. Jan–May 2024. Rafah rises to 31% as people are pushed south. The archive's centre of gravity reaches its southernmost point in February 2024, the month Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis was besieged. May 2024 alone has 731 Rafah records.
  3. Jun–Sep 2024. After Rafah empties, the central area (Deir al-Balah and the central camps) peaks at 35%. Rafah falls to 8% and stays near 5% from then on.
  4. Oct–Dec 2024. The north returns to 61%. October 2024 is the busiest month in the archive (11,697 records), with 1,701 from the north alone. Hospital mentions peak in December 2024, when 1 in 7 records named a hospital; Kamal Adwan, the last functioning hospital in the north, is the most-named facility.
  5. 2025. "Aid" and "famine", near zero in the first year, rise together and peak in August 2025 (9.3% and 3.8% of records). "Children" peaks at 24.3% of records in May 2025. Video rises from 49% of records in 2024 to 62%.
  6. 2026. Intake has settled at about 2,400 records a month. Only 9% of this year's records are mapped so far, because geolocation lags behind collection.

2.Impacts

  • Posts outlive their channels. 28 of the 31 collections have stopped growing: the channel went quiet, was banned, or its rescue covered a fixed period. Their posts are still here, each with its caption, posting time and source.
  • Events can be checked against a second record. The archive's peaks line up in time with independently reported events: Nasser in February 2024, Kamal Adwan in December 2024, famine in August 2025. Two independent records agreeing is stronger than either alone.
  • Places can be checked. 1,973 records are geolocated to an exact site and checked; of mapped hospital records, 941 (13%) are. These are leads an investigator can follow back to the footage.
  • The record has a human cost. Three collections come from the accounts of journalist Anas Al Sharif, who was killed in an Israeli strike in Gaza City on 10 August 2025, along with other journalists.[2] The people who film are themselves at risk, and any channel can stop without warning.
  • The record leans on a few hands. The three largest collections hold 45% of records, and in 7 months a single collection supplied over half. A fall in volume can mean a channel ended, not that less happened.

3.How it is useful

  • Journalists and researchers: find footage by place, month or caption word, then go to the original record on the archive. Start with Footage and Words.
  • Investigators and lawyers: use exact-site records as leads for verification. Posting time is not event time, and a caption is testimony. Start with Hospitals and the timelapse.
  • Health, aid and rights workers: set the hospital and famine timelines beside public reporting to show when attention, and conditions, shifted.
  • Educators and the public: a plain overview with its limits stated, from the home page to this one.
  • Archivists and volunteers: the gaps are visible. Most 2026 records are unmapped, West Bank records are not yet geolocated, and 966 records have no posting date. See Keepers and Numbers.
  • Data users: every page runs on open CSV and JSON files with a refresh script and a data dictionary, in the GitHub repository (MIT licence).

Limits
  • A record is a post, not a casualty count. Unfilmed or lost events are absent.
  • Volume reflects how the archive was collected as well as what happened.
  • Captions are the posters' own words: testimony, not verified findings.
  • Where something was filmed is not always where it happened.
