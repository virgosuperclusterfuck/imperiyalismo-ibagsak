What gets documented

The words people write under their footage are a record of attention: each month, what felt most necessary to say. Twelve of those words, laid out as a landscape, show how the subject of the record shifted across three years. Scroll, and the ridges light up one story at a time.

How to read it

A landscape of words

Each ridge is one word. Time runs left to right, from October 2023 to now. The height of a ridge is how much of that month's footage carried the word in its caption.

A tall peak means a month when that word was everywhere.

Children

The word that never falls flat

No word in the archive is as constant. At its height, in —, — of everything posted mentioned children.

It is the tallest ridge in the landscape, and it never drops to the floor.

Aid & famine

Two words rise together

In the first year, "aid" and "famine" barely appear. Then they climb side by side, and peak in the same month, —: the month a famine was formally confirmed in Gaza City.

That month, — of posts spoke of aid and — of famine.

Journalists

When the filmers become the story

Many of the people behind these cameras were journalists. The word itself peaks in —, at — of all posts: a month when their own names filled the captions.

Hospitals & ambulances

The same month, twice

"Hospital" and "ambulance" crest together in —, the end of the siege of Kamal Adwan, when — of all posts named a hospital.

A caution

What a word search can't see

This is plain text search on translated captions. "Hunger" is not counted as "famine"; a child named in a caption is not counted as "children". The ridges show where attention went, not how much happened.
