What gets documented

The words people typed under their footage, written in a hurry, on a cracked screen, sometimes in the dark, are a record of what felt most urgent to say.

Written from the Archive Genocide records · scroll slowly; the figure lights up as you read

In short

Twelve words that recur in captions, laid out as a landscape over three years. "Children" is the highest ridge and never falls flat. "Aid" and "famine" barely exist in the first year, then rise together. "Hospital" and "ambulance" crest in the same month, at the end of the siege of the north.

How to read the figure

1.A landscape of words

Each ridge is one word. Time runs from left to right, October 2023 to now. The higher a ridge rises, the more of that month's footage carried the word in its caption.

A peak is a month when everyone was saying the same thing.

Children

2.The word that never falls flat

No other word is as constant. At its highest, in —, — of everything posted mentioned children.

It is the tallest ridge in the landscape, and it never drops to the floor. People filmed their children, and other people's, more than anything else.

Aid and famine

3.Two words that rise together

In the first year, almost nobody writes "aid" or "famine". Then the two words climb side by side and peak in the same month, —, the month famine was formally confirmed in Gaza City.[1]

That month — of posts spoke of aid and — of famine.

Journalists

4.When the people filming became the story

Many of the people behind these cameras were journalists. The word itself peaks in —, at — of all posts: a month when their own names filled the captions.

Hospitals and ambulances

5.The same month, twice

"Hospital" and "ambulance" crest together in —, at the end of the siege of Kamal Adwan, when — of all posts named a hospital.

A caution

6.What a word search misses

This is plain text search on translated captions. "Hunger" is not counted as "famine". A child called by name is not counted as "children". The ridges show where attention went, not how much happened.

What this can't tell you
  • One post can carry several of these words, so the ridges don't add up to anything.
  • Months are partly shaped by which channels were being saved at the time.
  • A word's frequency measures attention. It is not a count of events.
Notes
  1. The Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC) confirmed famine in Gaza Governorate in August 2025.
