Where the footage points

People film where they are. Follow three years of footage across the map and you are following people as they were pushed from one end of Gaza to the other, and back.

Written from the Archive Genocide records · scroll slowly; the figure moves with the text

In short

Each geolocated post is a dot on the map. Stack the dots by date, oldest on the floor and newest at the top, and the archive becomes a column of time. Its shape tells a plain story: the cameras start in the north, move south to Rafah, crowd into the middle, then return north in October 2024 and stay there.

Oct – Dec 2023 · looking down from above

1.It begins in the north

From above, the figure is simply a map, north at the top. In those first weeks Gaza City and the north hold — of everything filmed. Al-Shifa. Jabalia. Beit Hanoun.

The south appears mostly as the place people were told to run to.

Jan – May 2024

2.The cameras move south

People went where they were told, and they kept filming. By spring, Rafah, the strip's last edge, crowded with tents, holds — of the record.

In May 2024 alone, — posts come from Rafah, as the assault there begins.

Jun – Sep 2024

3.The crowded middle

Then Rafah almost goes quiet. The dots gather in the middle of the strip instead, in Deir al-Balah and the central camps, where people had run next. They hold — of that summer's footage.

Oct – Dec 2024 · seen from the side

4.North again

Turn the column on its side and time runs upward. In October 2024 the north was besieged, and the record swings back: — of that season's footage is filmed there.

October alone brings — posts from the north. No month and place anywhere in the archive is more crowded.

2025 – 2026

5.And it stays north

From then on the north holds — of everything filmed. Rafah, once a third of the picture, falls to —.

The map of the record is a map of where people still were, and still had a phone.

All of it, turning slowly

6.Read it carefully

Only — of these dots are pinned to an exact building. The rest mark a town, a camp, a street.[1]

A dot is a post, not a death. Where the column thins, it may be that less happened, or that the people filming were gone.

What this can't tell you
  • Where people filmed is not the same as where things happened. Some places were too dangerous to film, or had no signal.
  • North and south here are bands of latitude, drawn at Gaza City, Deir al-Balah, Khan Younis and Rafah.
  • Mapped posts cover the Gaza Strip; the West Bank footage in the archive has not been mapped yet.
Notes
  1. The archive places each post at one of four levels: the town a caption names, the neighbourhood or camp, a named building, or an exact site worked out from the footage. Approximate dots gather on town centres, which is why some areas look like bright knots.
