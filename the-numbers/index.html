A.The rhythm of posting

Counted day by day, the archive takes in a median of — posts a day, but the average is pulled up to — by days when everything happens at once. Day-to-day swings are large: the coefficient of variation is —.[1] On — days, nothing at all was saved.

The busiest day stands apart from the rest. Almost all of its posts come from two collections gathered by the same group of volunteers, which suggests a batch re-posted at once rather than a day when far more happened.[4] A spike in this record can be an archiving event as easily as an event on the ground.

B.What kind of record

Year by year, the record has shifted from photographs toward video, while the share that can be mapped has fallen, partly because the newest posts have not yet been through the slow work of geolocation.

C.Where the weight sits

Take the average latitude of each month's mapped posts and you get the archive's centre of gravity. Its swing south in early 2024, toward Rafah, and its jump north in late 2024 trace the same displacement as paper 1, reduced to one line.

Some words rise and fall together month by month. Correlating the monthly share of every pair of the twelve caption words shows which subjects arrive as a package.

Aid and famine are the tightest pair. School, airstrike, ambulance, camp and drone form a second cluster, the vocabulary of bombardment of the places people sheltered.

F.The quality of the record

What this can't tell you
  • Every number here describes the archive: what was posted and saved. None of it is a count of events, injuries or deaths.
  • Rhythms and concentrations partly reflect how the archive was built, channel by channel.
  • Correlation between words says they rise together, not that one causes the other.
Notes
  1. Coefficient of variation: the standard deviation of daily posts divided by their mean. Days are counted from the first day with any post to the last complete day.
  2. Squares are about 1 km on a side (0.009° of latitude by 0.0105° of longitude). Gini runs from 0, every square equal, to 1, everything in one square.
  3. Herfindahl index: the sum of each collection's squared share of the archive. Its inverse is the number of equal-sized collections that would give the same concentration. Single-source dependency uses the twelve largest collections, which cover most of every month.
  4. Checked against the archive's per-collection counts for that day: two Evidence Task collections account for nearly all of its posts.
