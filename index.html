An introduction, and five short papers

What was kept

Since October 2023, people in Gaza have held up their phones and filmed what was happening to them. Many of those posts are gone now. Some were saved.

Written from the Archive Genocide records · about five minutes to read

In short

People in Gaza filmed their streets, their hospitals, their children, and posted what they filmed so that someone, somewhere, would see it. Channels were banned. Accounts went quiet. Volunteers copied what they could before it vanished. This page introduces what they saved and five ways of reading it.

  1. The archive holds — videos and photographs, each with the words its poster wrote.
  2. The busiest month was —: — posts, as the north was besieged.
  3. Only — of the records can be placed on a map at all.

1.One post at a time

Every record began as a person deciding to film. Some were journalists. Most were not. A father outside a hospital. A teenager on a rooftop. A woman walking through what used to be her street, narrating so she would not forget.

What the archive keeps is the post itself: the video or photograph, the caption its poster wrote, the moment it went up, and the channel it came from. — of them are video. None of it is a verdict. It is testimony, kept so that it can be looked at again.[1]

2.Month by month

Stand the months side by side and you get a skyline. Each tower is one month; its height is how many posts were saved from it.

The skyline rises and falls with the war, and also with the work of keeping it. When a big channel went dark, its months shrink here too. The fourth paper, Who keeps the record, is about exactly that.

3.The words people chose

The captions were written fast, in the moment, by the people holding the camera. Read enough of them and the same words keep coming back.

More children than airstrikes. More camps than mosques. You can read the war in what people felt they had to name.

4.Where they stood

— records can be placed on a map. Most of those only to a town or a camp. Pinning a video to one building takes hours of patient work: matching a broken minaret, a water tower, the line of a street, frame by frame.[2]

What this can't tell you
  • A post is not a casualty count. Many terrible days were never filmed, or the film was lost.
  • The archive only holds what someone managed to save. Its gaps are part of the story.
  • Captions are the posters' own words. They are testimony, not verified findings.
Notes
